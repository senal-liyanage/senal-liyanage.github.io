Research practice

Model molecular behavior, interrogate the trajectory, test the interpretation.

My work spans molecular simulation, electronic-structure analysis, and scientific software. Across those areas, the common goal is to turn computational output into reproducible evidence rather than treating calculation or visualization as the endpoint.

Research areas

Methods connected to concrete computational work.

These areas describe how I work, not just broad topics of interest. Each is tied to public case studies, code, or a research record.

01 · Molecular simulation

Molecular dynamics, solvation, and interfaces

I use atomistic simulation to examine molecular motion, solvation, structural change, and interfacial organization. Analysis emphasizes explicit definitions—cluster membership, radial structure, hydration, orientation, contacts, and related trajectory-derived observables—so conclusions can be traced back to measurable quantities.

NaCl dissolution case study Trajectory-analysis code

02 · Electronic structure

Ensemble TDDFT and spectral interpretation

Electronic-structure calculations become more useful when the analysis connects excited-state output to spectra and molecular geometry. The public qctddft workflow parses Q-Chem TDDFT results, constructs broadened spectra, identifies spectral regions, assigns states, and clusters structures to recover representative geometries.

Explore qctddft

03 · Scientific software

Reproducible analysis as part of the research method

I build small, inspectable tools around recurring scientific tasks rather than leaving analysis logic embedded in one-off notebooks. Public examples include an installable Python CLI, trajectory-analysis utilities, molecular-file processing, environment definitions, tests, and CI checks.

Selected software

04 · Scientific computing

Batch, GPU, and HPC-oriented workflows

Many molecular calculations are workflow problems as much as modeling problems. My public code includes command-line pipelines designed for batch execution and staged AMBER helpers that separate minimization, heating, equilibration, and GPU production dynamics into repeatable steps.
