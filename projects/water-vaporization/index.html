Water slab vaporization

A molecular-simulation example paired with an illustrative browser visualization of water leaving an initially compact liquid slab.

Model contextTIP4P/2005 water slab
SimulationOpenMM
VisualizationJavaScript canvas
PurposeCommunicate phase-like behavior
Important distinction The browser animation is illustrative rather than a rendering of trajectory coordinates. It communicates the slab-to-vapor concept while the simulation model provides the scientific context.
01 / QUESTION

How can a familiar phase-change idea be communicated at molecular scale?

A liquid slab provides a simple geometry for thinking about molecules moving away from a dense liquid region into vapor space. The scientific problem and the communication problem are related but not identical: one concerns the simulation model, while the other concerns how to make its qualitative behavior legible.

02 / MODEL

Finite water slab with vapor space.

I simulated TIP4P/2005 water in OpenMM using a finite slab geometry with vapor space along the vertical direction. The high-temperature behavior is the easiest regime to use for a visual explanation because molecules increasingly occupy regions away from the original liquid band.

03 / VISUALIZATION

Keep the communication layer separate from the simulation evidence.

The blue/orange particle animation is intentionally schematic. Blue points remain near the original liquid region while orange points occupy the vapor space. It is useful as an explanatory interface, but it should not be mistaken for raw molecular coordinates or a trajectory renderer.

This distinction is now explicit because scientific visualization is strongest when the viewer can tell which elements are measured, simulated, summarized, or illustrative.

04 / LIMITATIONS

This is not a boiling-point calculation.

A nanoscale slab with finite sampling is not equivalent to a macroscopic pot of boiling water. This example is intended to communicate molecular motion and phase-like behavior, not to report a measured boiling point, vapor pressure, or equilibrium phase boundary.

05 / ROLE IN PORTFOLIO

A visualization study, not a flagship research result.

The value of this piece is the combination of simulation context, scientific caveats, and browser-based visualization. More quantitative case studies are presented separately so that illustrative communication does not compete with research evidence.
